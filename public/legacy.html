📜 The Five Rules

read before every upgrade
  1. One chain at a time. Finish it before starting the next. At Engineers, needs stop being single goods and start needing several buildings feeding one product — so it feels like everything's happening at once. It isn't; you're just trying to build too many chains at the same time. Build one need's whole chain, watch the good pile up in storage, confirm the need shows green on the house, then start the next need. Never have more than one unfinished chain on the go.
  2. Meet the wants. They are income, not polish. A satisfied want raises the population cap and the tax per house — not just happiness. Skipping wants to rush the next tier is why money is always tight: you climb faster but earn less per house. Wide and fully-fed beats tall and half-fed.
  3. Build the supply before the demand. When one island feeds another island's needs, get it built and actually shipping in real quantity before you build the thing that relies on it. Rum should be arriving at Crown Falls in bulk before you upgrade Crown Falls to the tiers that drink it.
  4. Build the chains before you upgrade — but accept the workers only arrive when you upgrade. You can't fully stock up in advance, because the buildings that make the new goods need the new tier's workers, and those workers only appear when you upgrade the houses. So there's always a gap between upgrading and the new goods actually flowing. You keep that gap short and small — you can't remove it (see the Worker-Gap Box).
  5. Pollution affects the whole island, not just the area near the factory. Heavy industry lowers the happiness and attractiveness of the entire island wherever you put it — a far corner saves nothing. It's all-or-nothing: the dirty industry is on this island, or it isn't. For this save (no rivals to compete with): keep each island self-sufficient with its own heavy industry, and cancel out the pollution's happiness penalty by over-supplying needs, wants, and beauty. The "dead factory island with no residents" trick only pays off when you're competing with other players for attractiveness — you aren't, and it wrecks the self-sufficient-island feel you actually enjoy. Not worth it. Take the small happiness hit and cover it with luxury goods. (Beauty always buys back the tourists — attractiveness is a running total. Happiness is the one thing beauty can't fix; only removing the pollution does that, and you're choosing not to.)

⚠️ The Worker-Gap Box

the thing that makes you panic at each new tier

The trap: you upgrade houses to a new tier → the new needs appear straight away → but the buildings that meet those needs can't run until they have the new tier's workers, and those workers only exist because you upgraded. On top of that, some needs rely on other goods from the same tier (Artisans want cans; the cannery that makes cans needs windows; windows are also an Artisan good). So entering a tier can mean you need two chains running, one feeding the other, and neither can get workers until the moment you upgrade.

You can't make the gap zero. Keep it short:

  • Upgrade a few houses at a time, not a big block — a small worker shortage means a short gap.
  • Buy the good from a passing trader to cover the gap while the chain gets going.
  • Build the feeder chain first — get windows running before you rely on the cannery.
  • Let your money recover before upgrading the next batch. Money dips then climbs back = right pace. Money only dips = too fast, stop upgrading.

📐 Leave Space For This

grid houses, farms round the edge

Leave these gaps from the very start so DLC buildings you haven't unlocked yet drop straight in — no tearing anything down later. Unlocking them is the fun part; the space is already waiting when they arrive.

Farms (round the island edge)

  • Crop farms (fields): the Tractor Barn OR Fertiliser Silo (Seeds of Change / Bright Harvest) must sit directly adjacent — touching the farmhouse or its modules. → Leave one open tile touching every crop farmhouse. Both modules roughly double output (check panel). One module per farm.
  • Animal farms (sheep/pig/cattle): the Silo (Bright Harvest) only needs to be within the farm's radius — looser. → Keep radius space clear, don't pack another farm hard against it.
  • Silos eat grain/corn/teff; tractors/fertiliser eat fuel/fertiliser — each module needs its own road link to a warehouse. Leave a road edge reachable.

New World — reserve the centre for the Hacienda

  • Develop the New World just far enough to unlock the Hacienda (Seeds of Change) — then build the island around a reserved central block.
  • Start plantations at the edges, leave the middle open, so when the Hacienda unlocks it drops into the centre with its fertiliser works and buildings in range. No teardown.

Capital (Crown Falls) — reserve for beauty + skyline

  • Grid the houses, but leave green gaps for parks/ornaments (attractiveness) and a reserved block for skyscrapers (The High Life) — Investors go vertical there later.
  • Heavy industry lives on the same island (Rule 5 — pollution is island-wide, so a separate corner saves nothing). Give it its own grid zone away from the pretty core for your own visual sanity, not for a mechanical bonus. Cluster it so trade-union coverage and roads are clean.
  • Offset the island-wide pollution with over-satisfied wants + dense beauty, not by exiling the industry.

🗺️ The Phase Plan

pre-flight lists — complete before the upgrade

Each tier is a pre-flight list you complete before pulling the upgrade, not after. Numbers come from the calculator; needs come from the panel.

Phase 1 — Old World feeder island (keep it lean, forever)

  • Purpose: timber, clay/bricks, iron/coal/steel. A Worker-tier feeder. Nothing more.
  • Farmers → Workers. Build wide, meet needs, grow the base.
  • Leave farm-module space now (above) even though Bright Harvest unlocks later.
  • Seat of Power (Palace): reserve a block the moment you can. It gives island-wide buffs — including ones that ease the pollution/happiness balance on a self-sufficient island. Start it early, feed it slowly, ignore it between deliveries.
  • Sunken Treasures: the diving bell is available as a bonus raw-material source; use it if a resource is tight, don't build the island around it.
  • Push to Artisans ONLY far enough to unlock the Cape Trelawney expedition. Cross the gate with the minimum Artisan houses.
  • Cap Artisans by house count — use the downgrade arrow on residences to push houses back to Worker level if the Artisan count drifts up. Never let it reach the fur-coat trigger (residence panel: ____). Fur coats would force a permanent New World supply chain you're refusing to commit to. (Downgrading doesn't free workforce — it just keeps the count below the trigger.)
  • Which tier staffs the Steelworks? ____ — read the panel, and set your Artisan cap around it so steel production never runs short of the workers you're capping.
  • Build up a brick + timber stockpile before the expedition (time is free, so stocking up in advance is the cheapest kind of oversizing). Check each island's harbour storage limit before piling up — remember, extra inland warehouses don't raise that limit.

Phase 2 — Take the expedition · survey Crown Falls

  • Record from the survey: fertilities ____ · mineral nodes ____ · island size ____.
  • Settle. Immediately stand up local lumber, clay, steel. Crown Falls makes its own bricks and timber from turn one — cross-region shipping is a stopgap for the first few buildings only, never a supply line.
  • Ship only enough construction material for the first placements (one or two big ship-loads, not a trickle — oversize the producer, not the route).

Phase 3 — Crown Falls, lower and middle levels (most of your hours)

  • Full pyramid from scratch: Farmers → Workers → Artisans → (pause here).
  • One chain at a time. Meet every want at each tier before upgrading the next batch — that's the income you missed last time.
  • Once Crown Falls makes its own beams and windows, the Old World never needs to ship anything to it → switch off the Old World's export buildings, and the chains feeding them. This cuts their upkeep and stops them eating timber/coal/iron you no longer need. (No workforce benefit — the workers just go idle. The win is purely the cost you stop paying.)
  • Build beautiful. Reserve the skyline block (High Life, Phase 5), the green gaps (parks + Botanica garden), and the industry zone now.
  • Botanica: drop the Botanical Garden into a green gap once you want the attractiveness — it also gives collectible items the Item Transfer Depot will move between islands later.

Phase 4 — New World supply island (deep enough to feed, not a second capital)

  • Trigger: Crown Falls is about to want New World luxuries (rum, cigars, coffee, chocolate, cotton, oil).
  • Build it and get it actually shipping in real quantity BEFORE you upgrade Crown Falls to the tiers that consume it (Rule 3). Rum should be piling up at Crown Falls before the Engineers who drink it even exist.
  • Seeds of Change (Hacienda): develop the New World just far enough to unlock it, then keep the centre block clear and build plantations around it (see Leave Space). The Hacienda farms more tightly and produces more per tile — your supply island does more with less land and less fiddling. Central to this phase.
  • New World Rising is deliberately skipped — don't upgrade the New World's population beyond what supply needs. No full city there for its own sake.
  • Set up the goods route: big ship, plenty of storage at the Crown Falls end, fill the ship completely. Watch the stock at Crown Falls over 3–4 trips — falling = not enough being sent, sitting full = you're done, switch the producers off.

Phase 5 — Crown Falls goes tall (Engineers → Investors → Scholars)

  • Only now, with the New World supply already arriving.
  • Upgrade a few houses at a time, let your money recover between each batch. This is where you panic — the Five Rules and the Worker-Gap Box are here for exactly this phase.
  • Electrify, then delete half. Power roughly doubles a building's output. After you power up a chain, look at the production screen (or the calculator) and switch off or demolish the half you no longer need. Cutting that upkeep is the reward for electrifying — last time you paid for the power but never took the reward. Redo the calculator numbers every time you electrify, because power changes every output.
  • Scholars → Research Institute. Research builds up at a steady rate the whole time you have Scholars, so the sooner you get this running, the more total research you end up with. One of your two priority systems.
  • Skyscrapers (The High Life) go up in the block you reserved for Investors.

Phase 6 — Outposts: Enbesa, then Arctic (thin supply posts, last)

  • Supply-only. Enbesa for its goods, Arctic for gas/oil/furs as needed.
  • Keep them lean — they earn little, so don't over-invest. (Mail can change this — see Extra Systems.)

🔬 Extra Systems To Add Later

once the core game is running

Don't let these distract you early.

Docklands (one of your two priorities) — trades goods for goods, not for cash. A neutral trader turns up on a timer with unlimited cargo. Each contract swaps one export good for one import good at a fixed rate. You unlock more contracts by adding Exports Office modules (1 module = 1 more contract). You raise the module limit by exporting a lot of a single good to climb the specialty-exports ranks — and that limit is shared across all your islands, so pour your export volume into one good rather than spreading it thin.

Research Institute (the other priority) — lets you add fertilities and mineral deposits to islands. Use up everything Docklands and Research can give you before you ever settle a new island (your rule: trade and research before expansion).

Airship Item Transfer Depot — moves items (the socketable buffs with coloured borders), NOT goods. Saves you sailing a single specialist or trade-union item across the map. Upkeep is high (~423 a tick each) — build two (Old World + Crown Falls) and switch them off between transfers. Great for moving zoo/museum/cultural pieces and completing set bonuses. Outposts don't need one until they have a display building to receive items.

Mail / Postal Service (Empire of the Skies) — the one lifestyle need most people agree is worth it: it adds residents and income without making them consume anything else. Mail flown to a different island is worth more than mail delivered locally — the flying itself is what creates the value. Works best on small, densely-packed outposts (Enbesa, Arctic). This is the system that makes the airship platform's upkeep pay for itself, with item transfer coming along for free. It's a whole setup (post offices plus dedicated airship routes), so add it late — it's a money-booster, not a core system. Income per residence once a route runs: ____.

Beauty / Tourism — attractiveness is a running total, so if you pile on enough beauty the tourists keep coming even with smokestacks (income = attractiveness × 3.6: ____). The one thing beauty can't fix is the happiness hit pollution gives your residents — only removing the pollution fixes that, and for this save you're choosing to keep the industry and cover the happiness with luxury goods instead (see Rule 5).

🏭 Oversized Production

it's about capacity, not speed

There are two different situations. Don't mix them up:

  • Steady goods (bread, schnapps, sausages — used at a constant rate): build enough to cover what's used plus a bit of headroom, run it non-stop, and let the stored buffer smooth out the bumps. Only mild oversizing needed.
  • Burst goods (construction materials — used in sudden bursts): you place 40 buildings in 5 minutes, then build nothing for an hour. Build extra capacity, run it hard while you're NOT building, fill the storage, then switch half of it off and spend the pile. Sizing your brick production to the "average" rate is always wrong — too slow when you're building, wasted when you're not.

The cheap trick: switching a building off stops both its money upkeep and its worker upkeep. So oversizing means owning the extra capacity, not running it all the time. Fill up, switch off, sit on the stockpile.

Match the consumers exactly, over-build the raw end. Don't add a 4th beam factory "just in case" — 4 starved factories produce only 3 factories' worth of beams while you pay upkeep on 4. Put the extra capacity at the raw end (the furnaces) so steel bars stack up in storage and the factories downstream never run dry.

How much to ship (don't calculate — watch it): set the route to fill the ship completely and unload it all; let it run 3–4 full trips; then look at the stock at the destination. Rising or full = you're done. Falling = add producers or a second ship. Sitting steady in the middle = it works but with no spare margin. Over-shipping costs nothing; under-shipping is the only way to fail — so always send too much. Watch the stock at the origin too, or a fully-loaded ship will strip the origin island's own people bare.

Working out the ratio (use the Calculator tab, or by hand): pick a length of time that all your cycle times divide into cleanly (45s and 90s both go into 180s). Count the whole tons made vs eaten over that time. Example — 12 steel bars per 180s: a beam factory (45s) eats 4, a weapon factory (90s) eats 2, so spend your 12 exactly (3 beam / 2 beam + 2 weapon / 1 beam + 4 weapon / 6 weapon). Anything that changes a building's output — electricity, items, a worker shortage — breaks the ratio, so work it out again. Buildings with more than one ingredient eat one ton of each ingredient per cycle — work out the ratio for each ingredient separately; the scarcest one is your real limit.

🔁 How A Session Works

2-hour blocks

Every session, same three beats:

  1. OPEN (5 min) — read the Current Focus card. Glance at the balance and the storage bars. Fix anything that broke while you were gone before starting new work.
  2. WORK (~1h45) — pick one session type below. Do only that. Resist the urge to also fix the thing you noticed in the corner — write it in the Parking Lot instead.
  3. CLOSE (10 min) — run the Shutdown Check. Update Current Focus. Leave it clean.

The one discipline that matters: one job per session. The panic comes from doing five things at once. You have infinite sessions (no AI, no race) — so spend each on one thing done properly.

🎲 Session Types

pick one per sitting

🏗️ Build a tier

Grow one population level, properly.

  • Pick the tier you're upgrading. Open the calculator, get the building counts.
  • Build every chain that tier needs — including same-tier feeders (windows before cannery). One chain at a time, each confirmed producing before the next.
  • Upgrade houses a few at a time. If a need goes red while the new workers arrive, buy that good from a trader to cover the gap.
  • Done when: the batch is stable, all needs green, balance recovered. Stop there — don't start the next batch tired.

⚙️ Optimise one chain

Take one production line from "works" to "clean."

  • Pick one good. Open the production screen + calculator.
  • Check the ratio (whole tons over a shared window). Cut starved surplus buildings. Oversize the raw end so it banks stock.
  • If it's electrified: delete the redundant half and recheck.
  • Set/adjust the trade route if it crosses regions — load-to-full, watch destination over cycles.
  • Done when: the chain runs without you touching it, storage holding steady, nothing starving and nothing wasted.

🌳 Beautify

Pure attractiveness + tourist income. The low-stress session.

  • Fill reserved green gaps: parks, ornaments, Botanica garden, zoo/museum/botanical sets.
  • Check the attractiveness panel — watch the number and the tourist income climb.
  • Slot cosmetic-pack ornaments freely here (this is where they belong).
  • Done when: you've spent the beauty budget you wanted and the panel number is up. No wrong answers in this one — it's the session for a tired evening.

💰 Finance audit

Fix the bleed. Do this whenever balance feels tight.

  • Open the balance breakdown. Find the biggest upkeep drains.
  • Switch off anything producing into a full warehouse (stockpile goods you're not currently spending).
  • Check for starved/idle buildings paying upkeep for nothing.
  • Confirm every populated island is net positive on its own, or is deliberately fed by another.
  • Done when: per-tick balance is comfortably positive and you know why every big drain exists.

🚢 Expand a region

Open or deepen a supply island. Heavier session — needs the full 2h.

  • New World / Enbesa / Arctic in the phase order. Mark out the monument and Hacienda spaces first.
  • Stand up local construction materials before anything else.
  • Set the supply route back to Crown Falls. Get it visibly shipping before you build the demand.
  • Done when: the island supplies itself for building and one export is flowing.

🔬 Systems pass

Docklands, Research, Mail, Item Depot. The optimisation layer.

  • Pick one system. Set up or extend it.
  • Docklands: concentrate one export good up the pyramid. Research: queue fertilities/nodes. Mail: one outpost route. Depot: link two islands.
  • Done when: the system runs itself and you've noted its output in Current Focus.

🗺️ Expedition / quest run

The lighter, exploratory session.

  • Send a ship on an expedition, chase a timed quest, dive a wreck (Sunken Treasures).
  • Camera/photo quest? Check the tech reminders in the playbook — switch to GeForce Now if the pitch axis is locked.
  • Done when: the expedition's away or the quest's done. Low commitment — good when you've only got half a session in you.

✅ Shutdown Check

10 min, every session, no exceptions. This is the habit that lets the save survive gaps — skip it and you reopen into confusion.

🧭 The Point

You have unlimited sessions and no clock but your own. This save lasts because every sitting is one job, done, left clean — not the whole map juggled at once. The binge pace is what burned out the last saves; 2 focused hours that leave a readable game behind is what makes this the one that finishes.
